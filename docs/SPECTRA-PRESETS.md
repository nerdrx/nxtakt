# Spectra factory sounds

Spectra includes **185 factory entries: Init and 184 sounds**. The Studio expansion adds **64 sounds**, while all 121 earlier entries retain their names, settings and indices. Every new sound works with the eight built-in procedural tables; it needs no downloads, sample licenses or local wavetable cache.

## Find a sound

Choose a category in the preset browser, then select a sound. Bass patches are generally voiced for C1–C3; leads for C3–C5; pads and keys for C3–C6. These are useful starting registers, not keyboard limits. Sequence sounds start when you hold a note or chord and follow the project tempo. Release the keys to stop the new sequences: none enables Hold by default.

## Play the macros

All 64 Studio sounds assign Macro 1–4. They start at zero, the authored sound. Macro 1 consistently opens the filter. The other three reveal useful color, layer or texture changes; their precise jobs are listed below. Turn them gradually: wide changes in cutoff, FM and oscillator balance are performance gestures. Macro 4 is not always a rate knob; its audible purpose follows the patch. The FX panel exposes the integrated chorus, tempo-synced delay and reverb settings. Effects are part of each patch: bass foundations stay dry or lightly chorused, leads and plucks use measured echoes, and pads use room and hall tails.

Velocity always controls amplitude. Several keys and plucks also use velocity for brightness or FM bite; Ember Solo and Neon Flute respond to channel pressure. Mono and legato basses glide when notes overlap. FM and ring modulation use oscillator B even when its audible level is zero, so leave its table and pitch intact when exploring the patch.

## Studio expansion

These are original synthesized sounds. Names such as Rhodes, clav, flute and strings describe the intended playing character, not sampled acoustic or branded instruments. The 808-style Pressure Drop is a pitched bass voice, separate from NxTakt’s Drum Machine.

## Sound design and compatibility

The expansion spans subtractive, wavetable scanning, phase bend, sync, FM, ring modulation, layered harmonic voices and authored arpeggiator patterns. Long pads use slow envelopes and modest oscillator drift; plucks and keys use deliberately different transients and sustain shapes. Stereo widths and gains are set per sound, with mono bass foundations and conservative layer levels.

Preset loading restores all parameters and state before applying a patch, so changing from a busy sequence to a simple key does not leave an arpeggiator or modulation route running. Existing factory presets are unchanged. Projects save the actual instrument settings, rather than depending on the new bank’s position.

The new bank uses at most 64 parameter overrides per entry, within the existing preset format. Its category order is Bass 12, Lead 10, Pad 12, Keys 8, Pluck 10, FX 6, Sequence 6, alphabetical inside each group.
